# Stories of Care

Open Food Systems grows through small, human moments.  
This page is a living collection of quiet stories —  
simple acts of care that reflect the heart of the movement.

These stories are anonymous.  
They belong to no one and to everyone.  
They are reminders that dignity is built through everyday kindness.

## A Shared Meal
A man noticed his neighbor eating alone every evening.  
One day he knocked on her door with a warm dish and said,  
“I made too much — would you help me finish it?”  
They now share dinner once a week.

## The Extra Plate
A student living far from home cooked a simple meal.  
Before eating, she packed a second portion and left it on a bench  
where she knew someone often sat in the evenings.  
No note. No signature. Just food.

## The Market Conversation
A woman buying vegetables saw an older man counting coins.  
She quietly paid for his groceries and walked away  
before he could see who helped him.  
He left the market smiling.

## The Circle of Three
Three coworkers began meeting once a month to cook together.  
They started inviting others — not to “volunteer,”  
but to share food, talk, and support one another.  
Their small circle became a place of belonging.

## The Warm Drink
On a cold morning, a teenager bought two cups of tea —  
one for herself, one for the security guard  
who stood outside her building every day.  
He said it was the first time anyone had offered.

## The Garden Harvest
A community garden had more tomatoes than they could use.  
Instead of selling them, the gardeners filled baskets  
and left them at the entrance with a sign:  
“Take what you need. Leave what you can.”

## The Quiet Gesture
A man noticed someone sleeping outside his building.  
He didn’t wake him.  
He simply left a bag with bread, fruit, and water nearby  
and walked on.

## The First Circle
A woman invited two friends to talk about hunger in their city.  
They shared a meal, exchanged ideas, and made a plan  
to cook once a month for anyone who needed it.  
That was the beginning of their Community Circle.

## A Living Archive
These stories are not extraordinary.  
They are ordinary moments made meaningful  
by intention, kindness, and dignity.

Anyone can contribute a story —  
not by writing it here,  
but by living it out in the world.
