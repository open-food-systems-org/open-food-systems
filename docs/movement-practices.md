# Movement Practices

Open Food Systems is not only a movement of ideas —  
it is a practice of living with dignity, care, and connection.

These practices are invitations, not obligations.  
They are simple rhythms that help weave the movement into everyday life.

## Weekly: Share a Meal
Once a week, share food with someone —  
a friend, a neighbor, a coworker, or someone who feels alone.

A shared meal is one of the oldest forms of community.

## Monthly: An Act of Care
Choose one small act of care each month:
- offer a meal  
- support a local effort  
- cook with someone  
- help someone quietly  

Care grows through consistency.

## Daily: Notice Hunger
Take a moment each day to notice:
- who might be struggling  
- who might be isolated  
- who might need support  

Awareness is the beginning of action.

## Before Cooking: A Moment of Reflection
Pause for a breath before preparing food.  
Acknowledge the hands, land, and labor that made the meal possible.

Gratitude deepens connection.

## Invite Someone New
Once in a while, invite someone new to your table or your Circle.  
Movements grow through openness.

## Seasonal Gatherings
Each season, gather your Circle —  
to share food, reflect, and renew your commitment to dignity and care.

Seasons give rhythm to community.

## Learn and Unlearn
Make space to learn about:
- food systems  
- hunger  
- culture  
- community  
- dignity  

And unlearn assumptions that limit empathy.

## Practice Humility
Approach every act with humility.  
We help not because we are above others,  
but because we are connected to them.

## Care for Yourself
Sustainable care begins with rest, nourishment, and balance.  
A movement rooted in compassion must include compassion for oneself.

## A Living Practice
These practices are not rules.  
They are gentle invitations to live the movement —  
one meal, one act, one moment at a time.
