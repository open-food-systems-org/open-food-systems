# Community Circles

Open Food Systems grows through people — not institutions.  
Community Circles are small, local gatherings where the movement becomes real.

They can form anywhere:  
a home, a café, a park, a workplace, a kitchen table.

There is no membership, no hierarchy, no permission needed.  
Just people who care.

## What Is a Community Circle?
A Community Circle is a small group of people who meet to:
- share food  
- discuss ideas  
- support one another  
- take small acts of care  
- explore the movement together  

Circles are simple by design.  
They grow through trust, not structure.

## How to Start One
You don’t need experience or authority.  
You only need intention.

Invite:
- a friend  
- a neighbor  
- a coworker  
- someone who feels alone  
- anyone who shares the mission  

Begin with a conversation, a meal, or a shared act of care.

## What Happens in a Circle?
Anything that strengthens connection and dignity.

Some possibilities:
- sharing a meal together  
- discussing a page from the movement  
- planning a small act of care  
- cooking as a group  
- supporting someone in need  
- learning from one another’s experiences  

Circles are not meetings.  
They are gatherings of presence.

## Principles of a Circle
- **Everyone is equal.**  
- **Everyone is heard.**  
- **Everyone is welcome.**  
- **No one is pressured to speak or contribute.**  
- **Care is offered, not imposed.**  

Circles are built on dignity, humility, and kindness.

## Why Circles Matter
Large movements begin with small groups.  
Small groups begin with simple acts.  
Simple acts begin with one person who cares.

Community Circles create:
- belonging  
- resilience  
- shared responsibility  
- local action  
- human connection  

They are the roots of the movement.

## A Living Network
Circles can stay small or grow naturally.  
They can connect with other circles or remain independent.  
They can meet weekly, monthly, or whenever needed.

There is no right way — only the way that feels human.

## Start Where You Are
If you want to begin a Circle, start with one person.  
One conversation.  
One shared meal.

Movements grow through people who choose to gather.
