# Movement Ethos

The ethos of Open Food Systems is the inner light of the movement —  
the spirit that shapes how we show up, how we relate, and how we care.

It is not a rulebook.  
It is a way of being.

## The Spirit of the Movement
The movement is guided by a quiet, steady spirit:
- gentle  
- human  
- open  
- patient  
- grounded  

It moves at the pace of trust.  
It grows through presence, not pressure.

## Inner Commitments
These commitments are not obligations.  
They are the deeper intentions that guide our actions.

We commit to:
- honoring dignity in every interaction  
- approaching others with humility  
- listening before acting  
- caring without seeking credit  
- showing up with consistency  
- letting care be mutual, not one-directional  

These commitments shape the heart of the movement.

## The Posture of the Movement
The posture is how we carry ourselves —  
not physically, but emotionally and relationally.

Our posture is:
- open rather than closed  
- curious rather than certain  
- patient rather than hurried  
- present rather than distracted  
- humble rather than heroic  

This posture protects the movement from ego and urgency.

## Invisible Principles
Some principles are not written in lists.  
They are felt.

They include:
- **slowness** — allowing relationships to unfold naturally  
- **reciprocity** — giving and receiving with balance  
- **listening** — hearing what is said and unsaid  
- **restraint** — acting without overpowering  
- **presence** — being fully with the moment  
- **tenderness** — holding others with care  

These invisible principles shape the movement’s character.

## Ethical Grounding
The movement avoids:
- saviorism  
- hierarchy  
- spectacle  
- extraction  
- charity framed as superiority  

Instead, it embraces:
- solidarity  
- mutuality  
- shared humanity  
- dignity-centered care  

Ethics are not an add-on.  
They are the soil the movement grows from.

## Holding Power Lightly
Power exists in every human interaction.  
The movement holds it lightly.

We:
- share power rather than accumulate it  
- decentralize rather than centralize  
- invite rather than instruct  
- support rather than direct  
- uplift rather than overshadow  

Power is used only in service of dignity.

## Who We Are When No One Is Watching
The ethos is the movement’s inner compass.  
It guides us in quiet moments —  
when we share a meal, offer care, or sit with someone in need.

It reminds us that the movement is not defined by what we build,  
but by how we show up.

The ethos is simple:
**Be human.  
Be humble.  
Be present.  
Let care lead.**
