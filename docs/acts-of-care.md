# Acts of Care

Open Food Systems is not only a movement of ideas — it is a movement of people.  
And people change the world through small, human acts of care.

These acts are not charity.  
They are not saviorism.  
They are gestures of solidarity, dignity, and shared humanity.

## Offer a Meal
Invite someone to share food with you — a neighbor, a coworker, a stranger who feels alone.  
A shared meal is one of the oldest forms of connection.

## Share Quietly
If someone is struggling, offer support without making it a performance.  
Care is most powerful when it protects dignity.

## Cook Together
Prepare food with someone who might not have the means or the company.  
Cooking side by side builds trust and belonging.

## Support Local Efforts
Volunteer at a food initiative, community kitchen, or garden.  
Your presence matters as much as your time.

## Notice People
Sometimes the most meaningful act is simply seeing someone —  
acknowledging their presence, their worth, their humanity.

## Give Without Expectation
A meal, a bag of groceries, a warm drink —  
offered freely, without conditions, without judgment.

## Build Small Circles of Care
Invite friends, family, or neighbors to join you in simple acts of kindness.  
Movements grow through networks of compassion.

## Act With Humility
We help not because we are above others,  
but because we are connected to them.

## Let Care Be Contagious
One act inspires another.  
One moment of kindness becomes a ripple through a community.

## A Living Practice
Acts of care are not tasks to complete.  
They are ways of being — small, steady steps toward a world without hunger.
